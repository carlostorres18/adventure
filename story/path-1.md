## path-1
The village is five crooked houses and a bakery. Every window is dark except the bakery's, where warm light spills onto the cobblestones.

You glance through a dark window as you pass. A clock on the mantel says **9:47**. So does the clock in the next window. And the next.

Every clock in the village has stopped at the same minute.

A little sign on the house next door says KEEPER in faded paint.

[Knock on the keeper's house](#old-keeper)
[Go back to the crossroads](#crossroads)

## bakery
Rosa the baker opens the door in a cloud of flour. She looks at you, and for half a second her smile slips.

"You're the new keeper?" she says. "Then you'll want this. The tower stairs are black as a boot at night."

She hands you an old brass lantern. There are scratches on the handle, worn smooth, right where your fingers fall.

get: lantern

"There's pie, if you'd like some," she adds. "Blackberry. You always liked... I mean, *everyone* likes blackberry."

[Stay for a slice of pie](#pie)
[Ask her what she meant by "always"](#rosa-always)
[Thank her and head back out](#path-1)

## rosa-always
Rosa wipes the same clean spot on the counter, over and over.

"Don't mind me. I was nine the last time a keeper stood where you're standing. Big man, dark hair. Took a lantern just like that one and went up the cliff to watch the gulls go out." She won't look up. "That was a foggy night too. It was 1984. We never saw him again."

"But he's still next door," you say. "The sign..."

"Ask him yourself," Rosa says. "He answers the door on foggy nights. Only on foggy nights."

[Go knock on the keeper's door](#old-keeper)
[Stay for the pie after all](#pie)

## pie
The pie is warm, and the chair by the oven is soft. You close your eyes for just a moment.

You open them to the rumble of an engine. You're on the ferry. The crooked dock of Gull Island is coming out of the fog, and the lighthouse on the cliff is dark.

Somewhere out in the fog, a ship's horn sounds. Then another, closer.

Your brass lantern hangs from a hook beside you, swinging gently with the waves. You watch it for a long time, waiting for it to stop.

It doesn't.

THE END

## old-keeper
An old man opens the door just a crack. He's big through the shoulders, and his coat is wet, though it hasn't rained. When he sees your face, he grips the frame like the floor just tilted.

"Abernathy," he says at last. "Eleventh keeper of Gull Island."

"The tower door is locked, and the only key went down with my boat when she sank off the south beach. If you want that light lit, the key is in the wreck."

He studies you. "Unless you'd like to hear about the people who went missing. Most keepers don't ask. You... you look like the asking kind."

[Go back to the crossroads](#crossroads)
[Hear Abernathy out](#urban-legend)

## urban-legend
The old man lets you in. It's a fisherman's house: a couple of fishing rods, a net stiff with salt, and a calendar on the wall still open to **November 1984**. Water drips from his coat onto the floorboards and doesn't leave a mark.

His coat has a row of brass buttons with anchors on them. One button is missing.

"Sit down, please."

[Sit down in the old chair](#sit-down-old-chair)
[Remain standing](#remain-standing)
[Look at the photo on the wall](#keeper-photo)

## keeper-photo
There's one photo on the wall. A young Abernathy, about thirty, standing on the dock in front of a fishing boat. The name on her side is crisp and new: *Abernathy's Pride*.

At the very edge of the frame, half cut off, someone else is standing on the dock. Same coat as yours. Holding a brass lantern. Turned away from the camera, but you'd know that left pocket anywhere.

On the back, in faded ink, in **your handwriting**:

*2 Nov 1984. Don't eat the pie. The key is in the wreck. Keep the watch.*

You don't remember writing it. You weren't born in 1984.

Abernathy is watching you read it. "Sit down," he says again, gentler this time.

[Sit down in the old chair](#sit-down-old-chair)
[Stay standing](#remain-standing)

## sit-down-old-chair
As soon as you sit down, the chair cracks and collapses under you. It was a very old chair.

One leg was hollow. Out of it rolls a silver pocket watch, its hands stopped at **9:47**.

get: stopped watch

"Sorry," Abernathy says, but he doesn't sound sorry. He sounds like a man watching something happen exactly the way he remembered it. "That was mine. It stopped the minute she went down. I hid it in there so I'd quit looking at it. Keep it. You'll need it more than I do."

## remain-standing
You decide not to sit. A gust of wind rattles the shutters, and the old chair crumbles all on its own.

One leg was hollow. Out of it rolls a silver pocket watch, its hands stopped at **9:47**.

get: stopped watch

"Good thing you didn't sit down," Abernathy says. Then, quieter: "That was mine. It stopped the minute she went down. Keep it. You'll need it more than I do."

[Listen to the rest](#abernathy-warning)

## abernathy-warning
"You're not the first to come asking for the key," Abernathy says. "Last week, a young couple went looking for it. They followed a sound up the cliff trail, a sort of humming, like a crowd in another room. They never came back. Folks in the village say it was mermaids."

He leans in. "It wasn't mermaids. It's the gulls in the cave. And on a foggy night, this island's nights stack on top of each other like plates. Out there it's tonight. Down in my boat it's still 1984, and she's still going down, at 9:47, over and over."

He presses a lantern into your hands. "In case Rosa forgot."

get: lantern

## village-button
You hold out the brass button the crab gave up. The anchor on it is polished to a shine.

Abernathy takes it like it might burn him. He turns it over, and over.

"Forty years," he says. "I lost it climbing into the boat. I've been answering this door with my coat hanging open like a man who doesn't care how he looks on duty."

He threads it back on with fingers that shake. Then he does his coat up, every button, top to bottom, and stands a little straighter.

"Thank you, keeper," he says. Nobody has called you that yet. It fits better than you expected.

[Ask how to stop it](#village-truth)
[Go back to the crossroads](#crossroads)

## village-truth
Abernathy is quiet for a long time.

"Second of November, 1984. Fog since four. I didn't light the lamp. I went up to the cave to watch the gulls go out instead, and on the wall I saw eleven little painted people. The eleventh had my face."

"So I got in my boat and rowed out to the ship myself. Took the clapper off the fog bell so I could bang on her hull." He laughs, short. "Didn't make it."

He holds out his hand. It's wet and very cold.

"Somebody has to answer this door on foggy nights. Somebody has to tell the new keeper where the key is. I've done it for forty years. I'd like to stop."

[Take his hand](#village-loop)
[Pull away and run for the crossroads](#crossroads)

## village-loop
His hand is cold and wet. Then it's your hand that's cold and wet, and your coat is soaked through, though it hasn't rained.

You blink. You're standing in the doorway, holding the door open just a crack. Abernathy is walking away down the cobblestones, his coat buttoned to the top, and he doesn't look back.

The clocks still say 9:47. You know they will for a long time.

Some foggy night, a young keeper with a brass lantern will knock on this door and stare at you like they've seen a ghost. You'll know exactly what to say. You'll tell them where the key is.

THE END
