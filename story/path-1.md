by: Carlos Torres 

## path-1
The village is five crooked houses and a bakery. Every window is dark except the bakery's, where warm light spills onto the cobblestones.

You glance through a dark window as you pass. A clock on the mantel says **9:47**. So does the clock in the next window. And the next.

Every clock in the village has stopped at the same minute.

A little sign on the house next door says KEEPER in faded paint.

[Knock on the bakery door](#bakery)
[Knock on the keeper's house](#old-keeper)
[Go back to the crossroads](#crossroads)

## bakery
Rosa the baker opens the door in a cloud of flour. She looks at you, and for half a second her smile slips.

"You're the new keeper?" she says. "Then you'll want this. The tower stairs are black as a boot at night."

She hands you an old brass lantern. There are scratches on the handle, worn smooth, right where your fingers fall.

get: lantern

"There's pie, if you'd like some," she adds. "Blackberry. You always liked... I mean, *everyone* likes blackberry."

[Stay for a slice of pie](#pie)
[Ask her what she meant by "always"](#rosa-always)
[Thank her and head back out](#path-1)

## rosa-always
Rosa wipes the same clean spot on the counter, over and over.

"Don't mind me. It's just that the last keeper stood right where you're standing. Same coat. Same question." She won't look up. "He took the lantern too. He didn't stay for pie either."

"What happened to him?"

"Ask Abernathy next door. He's been awake longer than any of us."

[Go knock on Abernathy's door](#old-keeper)
[Stay for the pie after all](#pie)

## pie
The pie is warm, and the chair by the oven is soft. You close your eyes for just a moment.

You open them to the rumble of an engine. You're on the ferry. The crooked dock of Gull Island is coming out of the fog, and the lighthouse on the cliff is dark.

Somewhere out in the fog, a ship's horn sounds. Then another, closer.

Your brass lantern hangs from a hook beside you, swinging gently with the waves. You watch it for a long time, waiting for it to stop.

It doesn't.

THE END

## old-keeper
An old man opens the door just a crack. When he sees your face, he grips the frame like the floor just tilted.

"Abernathy," he says at last. "I kept that light for forty years."

"The tower door is locked, and the only key went down with my boat when she sank off the south beach. If you want that light lit, the key is in the wreck."

He studies you. "Unless you'd like to hear about the people who went missing. Most keepers don't ask. You... you usually do."

[Go back to the crossroads](#crossroads)
[Hear Abernathy out](#urban-legend)

## urban-legend
The old man lets you in. It's a fisherman's house: a couple of fishing rods, a net stiff with salt, and photos on the wall that look like they're from the 1920s.

"Sit down, please."

[Sit down in the old chair](#sit-down-old-chair)
[Remain standing](#remain-standing)
[Look closer at the photos](#keeper-photo)

## keeper-photo
Most of the photos are of boats. One is of a young lighthouse keeper standing on the dock, holding a brass lantern.

He has your face. Not someone who looks like you. *Your* face, down to the little scar on your chin.

On the back, in faded ink, in **your handwriting**:

*Don't eat the pie. The key is in the wreck. Keep the watch. Whatever you do, keep the watch.*

You don't remember writing it. You don't remember ever being here.

Abernathy is watching you read it. "Sit down," he says again, gentler this time.

[Sit down in the old chair](#sit-down-old-chair)
[Stay standing](#remain-standing)

## sit-down-old-chair
As soon as you sit down, the chair cracks and collapses under you. It was a very old chair.

One leg was hollow. Out of it rolls a silver pocket watch, its hands stopped at **9:47**.

get: stopped watch

"Sorry, son," Abernathy says, but he doesn't sound sorry. He sounds like a man watching something happen exactly the way he remembered it. "Keep that. You'll need it more than I do."

## remain-standing
You decide not to sit. A gust of wind rattles the shutters, and the old chair crumbles all on its own.

One leg was hollow. Out of it rolls a silver pocket watch, its hands stopped at **9:47**.

get: stopped watch

"Good thing you didn't sit down," Abernathy says. Then, under his breath: "That's new. You always sit."

[Listen to the rest](#abernathy-warning)

## abernathy-warning
"You're not the first to come asking for the key," Abernathy says. "Last week, a young couple went looking for it. They followed a sound up the cliff trail, a sort of singing, and they never came back. Folks say it was mermaids."

He leans in. "It wasn't mermaids. Every night the light goes out at 9:47. Every night the ferry brings a new keeper. And every night, the new keeper is *you*."

He presses a lantern into your hands. "In case Rosa forgot."

get: lantern

[Ask how to stop it](#village-truth)
[Back away and go find that key](#crossroads)

## village-truth
Abernathy is quiet for a long time.

"I was the first keeper," he says. "Forty years I kept that light burning. Then one night I fell asleep by Rosa's oven, the light went out, and a ship hit the rocks. I'd have given anything to go back and stop it."

He rolls up his sleeve. On his wrist is a small anchor tattoo, faded with age.

You look at your own wrist. The same anchor. Fresh ink.

"I got my wish," he says softly. "I've been going back ever since."

[Take his hand and let him show you](#village-loop)
[Pull away and run for the crossroads](#crossroads)

## village-loop
His hand is warm and dry. Then it's your hand, and it's old, and the knuckles ache.

You blink. You're standing in Abernathy's doorway, holding the door open just a crack. Outside, a young keeper with a brass lantern is staring at you like they've seen a ghost.

You know exactly what they'll say next. You know what you'll answer. You've said it a thousand times.

"Abernathy," you say. "I kept that light for forty years."

THE END
